Rae Health

Giving a clinical wearable platform its first real baseline

Product Manager Intern; Bristol, ME. June 2026 - September 2026

Overview

Worked on a live clinical wearable platform, defining foundational product metrics and using data analysis and customer research to validate the growth strategy.

What RAE is

RAE stands for Realize, Analyze, Engage. It is a wearable device and companion app designed to support people in recovery from substance use disorder. The wearable continuously monitors physiological signals, while machine learning models identify patterns associated with stress or potential craving. The app then provides interventions intended to help users de-escalate in the moment, rather than waiting until their next appointment.

The product serves three audiences: people in recovery, the treatment providers and care teams supporting them, and employers running wellness programs. The company is supported by the National Institutes of Health and UT Tyler, collaborates on research with UMass Medical School and Garmin, and holds two U.S. patents.

How the detection works

The wearable continuously measures accelerometry, skin conductance, skin temperature, and heart rate. Machine learning models analyze these signals to identify physiological patterns associated with stress and craving. When a relevant pattern is detected, the device vibrates and the app surfaces de-escalation tools, including guided breathing exercises, so support is available while the user is experiencing the state.

Detection is not entirely sensor-driven. Users can also log events manually, helping fill gaps in the data and providing labeled observations that can be used to evaluate and refine the models.

What the published research says

The detection system has been evaluated in peer-reviewed research. The model identified stress with 76% accuracy and craving with 69% accuracy, with false-positive rates of 33% and 28%, respectively. Clients reported greater awareness of their own physiological and behavioral patterns, while providers saw potential for more personalized care.

This research provided the context for the product metrics work. A product whose value depends on detection being reliable enough to act on needs to establish what is actually happening in practice: how many patients are actively using the system, how consistently they are being monitored, how much usable data is being generated, and how often users or care teams respond to detected events. The baseline analysis was designed to answer those questions.

What I did

  • Defined the company's first North Star metric and supporting metric tree (Reach → Activation → Retention → Clinical Engagement)
  • Analyzed event-level data (85 patients, ~91,000 detection events, 6 months) to compute the product's first measured baseline
  • Stress-tested the ROI model behind the company's growth strategy: traced each figure to source, formula, or "unverifiable", then recomputed break-even and reframed the claim before it reached prospects
  • Ran 6 customer interviews alongside usability studies to validate hypotheses before committing engineering capacity, and turned a gap in the acquisition funnel into a scoped backlog item
